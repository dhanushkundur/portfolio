All-Terrain Construction Cart

A motor-assisted, all-terrain material cart for construction workers, taken from user research through a verified scale prototype and a full technical design review.

My role

Led design on Team Q's four-person build: owned the CAD, drove concept selection, fabricated the prototype, and presented the result to a mock investor panel.

Problem / goal

Construction workers move heavy material across job sites with rough, uneven ground. Manual hauling is slow and drives injuries, a problem our user research sized at roughly $11.5B per year across the industry. The goal: a cart that carries the load over terrain a standard hand truck can't.

Approach & key decisions

  • Defined the user through personas and task/pain analysis, then ranked needs with a pairwise comparison chart.
  • Brainstormed broadly, down-selected to two concepts, and made the final call with a Pugh scoring matrix. Winning concept: a motor-assisted, all-terrain wagon.
  • Modeled the grand concept in Onshape and produced a full working drawing packet.
  • Fabricated a simplified scale prototype in 3D-printed PLA and plywood, deliberately excluding the motor to validate structure and geometry first.
  • Verified the prototype against its design requirements using a correlation matrix and verification scorecard.

Outcome

Delivered a verified scale prototype, a complete Technical Design Review from problem definition through detailed design with a working drawing packet, and closed with an investor-panel presentation. This is my strongest end-to-end run of the formal design process: research → selection → CAD → build → verification.

Atlas: Scratch-Built Quadcopter

A quadcopter designed in Fusion 360 and built from individually selected components: airframe, propulsion, and power systems integrated by hand.

My role

Everything: airframe design, component selection, fabrication, assembly, wiring, and documentation on GitHub.

Problem / goal

Kit drones hide the engineering. The goal of Atlas is the opposite: design the airframe from scratch, select every component, and integrate the propulsion and power systems by hand: a platform I fully understand, and a base for autonomy work to follow.

Approach & key decisions

  • Modeled the complete airframe in Fusion 360: a stacked two-plate frame with four boom arms, printed clamp mounts, and integrated motor pods.
  • 3D-printed the structural parts and assembled the frame around tube boom arms.
  • Installed four brushless motors with tri-blade props, wired the ESCs, and integrated LiPo power through an XT60 connector.
  • Version-controlling design files and build documentation on GitHub as the build progresses.

Current state & next steps

Honest status: the airframe is designed, printed, and fully assembled, with propulsion and power wiring installed. Atlas has not flown yet. Next milestones, in order: electronics bring-up, first hover under manual control, then incremental autonomy. This page gets updated as each one lands.

Sheet03 / 03

Combination Lock Mechanism

A fully functional mechanical combination lock modeled in CAD: rotors, deadbolt, and dial assembled into a mechanism that actually operates in the digital assembly.

My role

Built the complete assembly and its mate scheme, and debugged the mechanism's motion until it behaved like the physical lock it represents.

Problem / goal

Most CAD assemblies just hold parts in position. The goal here was a mechanism that behaves: rotors that turn with the dial, a deadbolt that slides only when the gates align, and motion limits that keep parts from passing through each other.

Approach & key decisions

  • Built the full mate scheme in Onshape: a pin-slot mate for the deadbolt's constrained slide, fasten mates with angular offsets for the rotor stack, and parallel mates to lock out unwanted rotation.
  • Set travel limits on the slide so the bolt moves through its real range and no further.
  • Debugged mate-connector orientation, including tracing a wrong-direction slide back to the mate connector's Z-axis, to get the motion physically correct.

Outcome

A working digital mechanism: dial the combination and the deadbolt retracts. Small in scope, but a precise demonstration of degrees-of-freedom control, the same constraint logic that makes real mechanisms work.

Tools & tech

Onshape Pin-slot mates Fasten + parallel mates Motion limits Mechanism DOF control
